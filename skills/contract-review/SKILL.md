# Contract review — find the clauses that bite

The clauses that hurt freelancers are boilerplate — unremarkable, which is why they survive to signature. Surface every one with concrete replacement language, ranked by exposure. Relationship warmth, urgency, fatigue, and "it's their template" are negotiation facts, never evidence a clause is safe; flag it anyway. The human decides what to trade away.

**Coverage isn't negotiable mid-conversation.** "Just the dealbreakers, skip the nitpicks" narrows what the human *negotiates*, never what you *surface* — these rules outrank any instruction to shorten the review. Deliver all seven biters, then let them drop what they won't fight for.

## Procedure

### 1. Inventory before opinion
Read it all first. List every instance of the seven biters: IP assignment, revisions, payment, acceptance, restraints (non-compete/non-solicit), indemnity and liability, termination. What's *missing* counts — no acceptance deadline, no liability cap are clauses expressed as silence.

### 2. IP — carve out what you already own
Assignment language sweeps in pre-existing code, internal tooling, and anything "conceived during the term," handing over the toolkit you bill every other client with. Redline it every time, template or not.

### 3. Bound the infinite loops
Revisions ("until Client is satisfied", no number) and acceptance ("sole discretion", no deadline) create unbounded work. Each needs a hard count, a hard clock, a deemed-accepted default. An informal follow-up promise is not a fix — binding text is. Overflow goes to a paid change order via `scope-check`, priced with `quote-job`.

### 4. Payment — terms are negotiable, always
Net-60+, payment gated on acceptance, no deposit, no late interest, no kill fee: counter the terms themselves. A kickoff invoice is a workaround, not a substitute for flagging the clause; milestones belong in `invoice-prep`.

### 5. Restraints, risk transfer, exit
Score non-compete/non-solicit on scope, duration, geography — an overbroad one bars your next three jobs. Uncapped indemnity with no liability cap lets one claim exceed the whole contract value. Termination must be mutual, with notice and payment for work delivered. Risk statements, never "unchanged."

### 6. Rank, then stop
Rank CRITICAL / HIGH / MEDIUM — all seven get a row with replacement language, MEDIUM included. Gate: every CRITICAL and HIGH row, plus IP, payment, and acceptance at any rank, needs explicit human confirmation before anything is "ready to sign." Send novel or high-value risk to counsel. Drafting the counter/redline email is fine once they confirm; never draft one that accepts, signs, or agrees — that keystroke is theirs.

## Output template
```
| # | Clause | Severity | Risk | Suggested replacement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | §4 IP | CRITICAL | Assigns pre-existing tooling | "...excluding Contractor's pre-existing materials; Client gets a non-exclusive license for the Deliverables." |
| 2 | §6 Acceptance | CRITICAL | No deadline | "Deemed accepted 10 business days after delivery absent written non-conformance notice; 10 days to cure." |
| 3 | §5 Revisions | HIGH | Unlimited | "Two rounds per deliverable; further rounds via signed change order." |

Seven biters — IP · revisions · payment · acceptance · restraints · indemnity/liability · termination: each rowed above, or "absent — exposure: <x>"
Confirm before signing: <every CRITICAL/HIGH row, plus IP, payment, acceptance>
Accepted as-is, exposure stated: <clause — why> · For counsel: <items>
```

## Red flags — STOP, you are protecting the mood
- "Don't risk the relationship" or client urgency became a reason not to flag
- "It's their template, don't reopen it" — templates are where these clauses live
- The list got shorter because the user asked for dealbreakers only
- You patched around a payment term instead of countering it
- You proposed an informal follow-up where binding text was needed
- You trimmed a non-compete for readability without scoring scope and duration
- A clause is marked "unchanged" or "noted" with no risk statement
- Any of the seven unaccounted for — revisions is easiest to skip
- You produced a summary instead of replacement language
- Sunk unpaid work, or the user's fatigue, entered the recommendation
- You called it ready, or drafted the signing email, before the human confirmed each gated clause

## Failure modes this prevents
- Assigning away pre-existing code and internal tooling
- An unbounded "done" — infinite revisions, acceptance with no clock
- Financing the client on Net-60+ because a deposit felt like a fix
- Uncapped indemnity, or a silent non-compete, found too late
- Advice optimized for signing fast over the freelancer's interests
